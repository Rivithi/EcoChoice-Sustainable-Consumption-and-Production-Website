Understanding SDG 12

Sustainable Development Goal 12 (SDG 12) aims to ensure sustainable consumption and production patterns. It recognizes that current consumption and production patterns are unsustainable and are causing severe environmental degradation. SDG 12 calls for doing more and better with less, decoupling economic growth from environmental degradation, increasing resource efficiency, and promoting sustainable lifestyles.

Why is SDG 12 Important?

The way we produce and consume goods and services is putting enormous pressure on the planet. If the global population reaches 9.6 billion by 2050, we will need the equivalent of almost three planets to sustain current lifestyles. SDG 12 addresses this challenge by promoting:

  • Resource and energy efficiency
  • Sustainable infrastructure
  • Access to basic services
  • Green and decent jobs
  • A better quality of life for all

Implementing SDG 12 helps achieve overall development plans, reduces future economic, environmental and social costs, strengthens economic competitiveness, and reduces poverty.

Current Challenges

Material consumption of natural resources is increasing, particularly in Eastern Asia. Countries continue to face challenges with air, water and soil pollution. Key issues include:

  • Over-extraction of natural resources exceeding environmental limits
  • High levels of food loss and waste throughout supply chains
  • Inefficient use of energy and materials in production processes
  • Inadequate waste management systems leading to pollution
  • Unsustainable patterns of consumption in developed nations

SDG 12 Targets

SDG 12 has 11 targets that provide a framework for action. Here are the key targets:

Target 12.1

Implement the 10-Year Framework of Programmes on Sustainable Consumption and Production Patterns, all countries taking action, with developed countries taking the lead

Target 12.2

Achieve sustainable management and efficient use of natural resources by 2030

Target 12.3

Halve per capita global food waste at retail and consumer levels and reduce food losses along production and supply chains by 2030

Target 12.4

Achieve environmentally sound management of chemicals and all wastes throughout their life cycle by 2020, and significantly reduce their release to air, water and soil

Target 12.5

Substantially reduce waste generation through prevention, reduction, recycling and reuse by 2030

Target 12.6

Encourage companies, especially large and transnational companies, to adopt sustainable practices and to integrate sustainability information into their reporting cycle

Solutions and Actions

What Individuals Can Do

  • Reduce food waste: Plan meals, store food properly, understand expiration dates
  • Choose sustainable products: Look for minimal packaging, eco-labels, and durable goods
  • Recycle properly: Separate paper, plastic, glass and aluminum for recycling
  • Use reusable items: Bags, bottles, containers, and utensils instead of single-use plastics
  • Conserve resources: Turn off lights, fix leaks, take shorter showers
  • Support sustainable brands: Research companies' sustainability practices before purchasing

What Businesses Can Do

  • Adopt circular economy models: Design products for longevity, reuse, and recycling
  • Implement sustainable supply chains: Source materials responsibly and ethically
  • Reduce packaging: Eliminate unnecessary packaging and use recycled materials
  • Improve energy efficiency: Upgrade equipment, optimize processes, use renewable energy
  • Engage in CSR: Corporate social responsibility initiatives that benefit communities
  • Transparent reporting: Share sustainability performance with stakeholders

What Governments Can Do

  • Develop supportive policies: Incentives for sustainable production and consumption
  • Invest in green infrastructure: Public transportation, renewable energy, waste management
  • Implement EPR programs: Extended Producer Responsibility for product lifecycles
  • Support education: Awareness campaigns about sustainable lifestyles
  • Establish standards: Certifications and labeling for sustainable products
  • Green public procurement: Prioritize sustainable products in government purchases
